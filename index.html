Gamified & Tested

How we brought on user engagement and tested the theory with user testing.

Overview

It started with two apps, one goal in mind

We were approached by the client to take an existing app, transform it to be similar to another one of their international sub-products, and then completely rework what both apps focused on enhancing the experience to drive customer engagement both in-app and in-store.

No, this was not an e-commerce product at all. There was no buying of products or services, even though there was a payment gateway, but rather indulging in what the service has to offer for its very broad customer base. Bringing the brand, their story, their offerings, and what they can do for you, to light.

Role
UX / UI Design with Product thinking
Focus
User engagement & retention
Interface
Mobile application
Tested using
Lyssna.com
Timeline
4-5 months
The challenge

An app a few customers had, but rarely engaged with

We set out to answer the big question the client had: Why were customers not interested in the app?

It wasn't the brand themselves; they were very well known and trusted. They brought the convenience and majority of the offerings many of their competitors were bringing to the table. So, the UXers coming into the project blind started looking at what the app already had to offer and how we could push their offering further to have it be more enticing and most of all, have a purpose beyond making a payment.

How we looked at the challenge
01 · What were the blockers and confusions?

We looked at the existing app, analysed what they wanted to transform it into, and got a vast understanding of what their competitors do and why they were succeeding.

This started with:

  • Current information architecture analysis
  • Card sorting to propose a new IA
  • Competitor analysis
  • Understanding the company structure & offerings
  • Workshops with a stakeholder, their marketing lead, and a contracted business man
02 · How can we use their existing reward system to drive customer engagement?

We can all admit being customers ourselves; we all want something out of this beyond the product and service on offer. Competitors are offering a much stronger loyalty & rewards program; we can't have the brand stay stagnant. But, as with many things, asking them to part with their ways is a bit of an ask. So, we use what they have on offer, and put a bit more meat to it.

This started with:

  • Workshops with their marketing lead
  • Understand what has been built & the mechanics of what is on offer
  • Where would this live in the app (IA)
  • What are the limitations
  • Would customers actually enjoy engaging & long-term participation (make them come back)
03 · Looking at the visual direction

Did the current visual style fit the brand? Absolutely. But any UX designer can tell you that functionality is more important than an app that just looks pretty. The graphics were superb but execution, accessibility, consistency, and discovery were the real challenges. So, what did we do?

This started with:

  • Explored various design styles
  • Analysis against the WCAG accessibility guidelines
  • Heuristic analysis of core journeys
  • Using AI for image generation and consistency of visuals
Heuristic analysis of the registration journey
The process

The organised chaos, and how we get to the end product

  • 01
    Facilitated workshops
    Going to client offices and discussing all the important aspects with all the important people. The workshops were very discovery heavy in the beginning, but later turned into review sessions for the iteration process.
  • 02
    Analysis & research
    Mapping out the full end-to-end journey of the existing application. Finding pain points, incorrect placement of features, fixing the chaos, and validating findings through small in-person testing and using an online platform like Lyssna to broaden our horizon.
  • 03
    Take a low hanging fruit to figure out the design direction
    Utilising our first one or two journeys we built to explore the visual direction we were heading for, whilst still building the product. This is where the design system started coming into play.
  • 04
    Build the core features
    "What does the app need to function?" we asked. Researching into how payment gateways operated, how to improve them, how we can translate a real-world action to a digital experience whilst still competing with aspects like Apple/Samsung Pay. The loyalty program was set to be the feature that tied in to payment whilst still bringing customers back, driving user engagement, and broadening their client base.
  • 05
    Exploring a CRM system
    Having an app is great, but letting your customers know about it and how they can engage with it is key. Planning a CRM system gives the brand the ability to own its customer engagement strategy end-to-end, without being locked into a third-party platform.
  • 06
    The build is done, now to test
    Using a site like Lyssna allowed us to test across the country & get insights. Validating the research we did, understanding user behaviour, and how we can adapt and iterate the designs to have it be easily accessed (easy discovery), match their expectations, and enjoy their experience.
  • 07
    Iteration is always a part of the journey
    After testing, we go back and apply the change if there's a valid reason to. We also see how this affects the design system, then neaten it all up and explain the decisions.
  • 08
    Handover time
    It's not just screens. It's explaining the decisions, doing write-ups of what is in scope and what is meant for the future (enhancements). Handing over everything from functional requirements to process flows, with the visuals to complement the business and development logic.
Deep dive

Loyalty & Rewards

Loyalty and rewards sat at the centre of the product strategy, but the app itself gave it almost no room to breathe. This section walks through how we unpacked the feature, what testing told us, and how we reshaped The Wheel of Goods and coffee loyalty into something people could actually find.

01 · Feature overview

What we were building

Module
Payments & Loyalty
Status
Ready for development
Purpose

Let users engage with the loyalty ecosystem end to end, including competitions, The Wheel of Goods rewards, a digital coffee loyalty stamp card, and linked partner loyalty cards, all from inside the app rather than scattered across separate touchpoints.

In scope
  • Rewards dashboard
  • Competitions listing
  • The Wheel of Goods game flow
  • Voucher history
  • Coffee loyalty stamp card flow
  • Linking external retail and banking rewards accounts
Not in scope
  • Gamification and prize weighting algorithms
  • Partner programme contractual logic
Success definition

A user can find, understand, and act on every reward the programme offers, competitions, The Wheel of Goods, coffee loyalty, and linked partner cards, without needing to be told where to look.

02 · User problem & goals

Why it wasn't working

Loyalty and rewards were treated as an add-on rather than a core part of the app. Coffee loyalty only ever existed in-store, with no presence in the app at all, and The Wheel of Goods only appeared once a user had already made a payment, as a reward for using the app rather than a destination in its own right. Neither feature had a real entry point, and the one moment they did surface worked against them.

Why the timing worked against it
  • It only ever appeared after a payment had gone through, right when a user wanted to leave, not linger
  • Not everyone has reliable data on-site or in-store, which limited how many people could ever reach it
  • Making a payment is a task people want to finish quickly, not pause partway through to spin a wheel
  • The prizes on offer weren't compelling enough to make the wait worth it

03 · The Wheel of Goods

The Wheel of Goods

The product's flagship engagement moment, and the clearest example of a good idea trapped behind the wrong entry point.

Started

The team's flagship engagement feature: a spin awarded after every payment, positioned as a thank you rather than a destination in its own right.

Tested

Participants were consistently drawn to the wheel the moment they saw it, but it surfaced right when they wanted to finish up and leave. Some chose to save their spin for later instead of playing there and then.

End goal

Give the wheel its own entry point, so it's something people choose to open, not something that only finds them once, right after paying.

Future thinking

A persistent rewards entry point in the main navigation, spin history and reminders for saved spins, and richer prizing tied to real value rather than one-off surprises.

Usability testing on the payment and spin flow confirmed the pattern directly: participants were more attracted to the wheel than to anything else in the payment flow, and when they didn't want to engage there and then, they would save it for later rather than skip it altogether. The appetite was never the problem. The moment was.
04 · Coffee loyalty

Coffee loyalty

A well-used habit in-store that the app never got to be part of.

Started

A physical, in-store stamp card only. No presence in the app at all, so the product had no way to reinforce or reward the habit digitally.

Tested

Discoverability here was a lower-severity issue than the wheel: people expect to explore a little to find loyalty features, and testing showed they generally did. The real gap was that there was nothing to check or track once they got there.

End goal

Move coffee loyalty into the app as a digital stamp card, so progress carries across visits and is visible without needing the physical card.

Future thinking

Automatic stamp detection tied to in-store payment, milestone rewards for completed cards, and surfacing coffee loyalty alongside the wheel as one connected rewards surface rather than two separate systems.

Testing also flagged that once users found the loyalty area, they could reliably tell coffee loyalty was part of the rewards programme, they just had nothing to act on there yet. That reframed the fix: less about discoverability, more about giving the feature something to actually do inside the app.
05 · Tying it together

Where it meets payments

Both features were historically bound to a single moment: the point of payment. That's not a reason to disconnect them from payments entirely, it's the opportunity. Payment is still the most natural, highest-frequency trigger for loyalty engagement in the app. The fix isn't removing that link, it's adding a second one.

Give The Wheel of Goods and coffee loyalty their own home in the app's navigation, so payment becomes one way in, not the only way in. A user who wants to check their stamp card or spin the wheel on a Tuesday afternoon, with no payment in sight, should be able to.
